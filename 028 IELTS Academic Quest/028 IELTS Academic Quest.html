🗺️ Your IELTS Test Day / テスト当日の流れ

Know the journey, and nothing can surprise you. 🎯 = rooms this reviewer trains.

1 BOOK → 2 CHECK IN → 3 🎯 LISTENING → 4 READING → 5 WRITING → 6 🎯 SPEAKING → 7 SCORES
🆕 2026 Format Update (Japan & Worldwide): Traditional full paper-based IELTS ended in mid-2026 (August 2026 in Tokyo & Osaka). Today you sit IELTS on Computer (~2 h 30 back-to-back, no breaks) — in selected centres, you can choose Writing on Paper to handwrite your essays while still doing Listening and Reading on screen.
2026年夏より日本国内のIELTSは原則コンピューター受験になりました（一部地域ではライティングのみ手書き方式も選択可）。

Speaking is always face-to-face with a certified examiner — either the same day (before/after) or on a different day up to 7 days before/after. Check your booking confirmation.

1

📝 Book IELTS Academic at an official test centre

Book IELTS Academic on Computer at an authorised test centre (plus Writing on Paper where available). Typical benchmarks: Undergraduate 6.0 (min 5.5) · Postgraduate 6.5 (min 6.0) · Teacher / Professional Registration (e.g. NZQA / Teaching Council NZ) 7.0 in every skill. Official computer centres also offer One Skill Retake within 60 days — avoid at-home remote tests if applying for licensing or visas.

大学進学（目安6.0〜6.5）も海外教員登録（例：NZ教員登録 各7.0以上）も IELTS Academic（公認会場受験） を選びます。1技能のみ再受験（One Skill Retake）にも対応。

2

🎒 Pack & arrive early

Bring the exact same valid ID (passport) you booked with. Into the test room: only your ID and water in a clear bottle with no label. Phones, watches, notes, and bags stay locked outside. Arrive early for check-in — eat and use the toilet first because there are no breaks.

持ち込みはパスポート（申込時と同じ身分証）とラベルなしの透明な水のみです。

3

🪪 Check in

Staff verify your ID, take a security photo (and finger scan at many centres), store your belongings, and guide you to your computer booth. Need help at any moment? Raise your hand quietly.

受付は全員同じ手続きです。困ったら静かに手を挙げましょう。

4

🎧 Listening room 🎯 — about 30 min, 40 questions

Put on your headphones and check the volume — if anything sounds unclear, raise your hand immediately. You hear 4 recordings, each ONE time only (everyday chat → public talk → student discussion → academic lecture). Use the short preview pauses for prediction. ⚠️ Crucial computer rule: enter answers directly on screen as you listen — at the end you get only 2 minutes to check (not the old 10-minute paper transfer time)!

コンピューター版は最後の見直し時間が約2分しかありません。聞きながら直接入力・選択しましょう。

5

📖✍️ Reading (60 min, 40 questions) → Writing (60 min, 2 tasks)

Runs straight on with no break. On computer, Reading splits passage + questions side-by-side with highlight/notes tools (no extra transfer time). Writing displays an automatic live word counter on screen with spellcheck OFF (or printed booklets if taking Writing on Paper).

リーディングは左右分割画面、ライティングは語数が自動表示されます（スペルチェック機能はなし）。

6

🗣️ Speaking room 🎯 — 11–14 min, one examiner

Show the same ID and wait to be called. Part 1 (4–5 min) personal interview → Part 2 cue card, 1 min keyword notes, speak 1–2 min, plus 1–2 short rounding-off questions → Part 3 (4–5 min) two-way discussion. Recorded as standard. Being stopped politely for time is normal — it never means you failed.

Part 2の直後に短い締めくくりの質問が1〜2問入ります。時間で止められても減点ではありません。

7

🏁 Finish & scores

Stay seated until dismissed and report any room/equipment issue before leaving the centre. Computer results usually arrive within 1–2 days (longer for Writing on Paper): 4 skill band scores + 1 overall band score (1–9).

コンピューター版の結果は通常1〜2日で届きます（4技能＋総合バンド1〜9）。

Part B · Sentence Completion — Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS

📄 Script H (2 speakers) — open AFTER you answer

Dr. Patel: Come in, Kenji. Let us check the four stages of your river microplastics investigation before lab work starts next week. Have you finalized Step One yet?
Kenji: Yes, Dr. Patel. I originally planned to borrow plastic bottles from the canteen, though obviously plastic containers could contaminate the water! So in Step One, we collect river water using clean glass jars [B]. Then in Step Two, back in the lab, we pour each sample through a fine metal filter [C] to trap tiny fibres.
Dr. Patel: Good thinking avoiding plastic bottles. Watch out in Step Three, though — sand grains will also sit on that filter alongside any plastic. How are you separating heavier grit before counting?
Kenji: Right — in Step Three we warm the trapped material gently in a salt solution [E] so lighter plastic particles float to the surface. Then in Step Four, we identify the polymer types under an optical microscope [G]. Overall, published studies show washing synthetic clothing [1] is the biggest source of river fibres, and heavy rain makes fibre levels double [2] soon after storms. Also, during lab sessions everyone must wear cotton coats [3] so our own sleeves do not shed synthetic fibres!

📄 Script E — open AFTER you answer

Good morning everyone. In today's environmental design lecture, we are looking at urban rooftop gardens and how cities build them safely. First, why are city councils investing in green roofs? Some people assume the main benefit is lower heating bills in winter, though in practice winter insulation gains were fairly small. What made a dramatic difference instead was summer cooling: plant layers kept top-floor rooms significantly cooler during heatwaves [B]. In addition, the soil beds absorb heavy rainfall, which reduces pressure on street drains [D] during storms. Harvested herbs are usually shared among residents rather than sold for profit. Turning now to how a rooftop garden is built, safety comes first. Before any soil is lifted up, the roof structure must be checked by an engineer [1] to confirm the load limit. To keep weight down, the soil layer is typically just fifteen (15) [2] centimetres deep, rather than the fifty centimetres needed for large trees. Because of that shallow soil, the most reliable plants are hardy herbs and shallow-rooted flowers [3]. Finally, one unexpected maintenance problem: insects are quite rare high above the street, so pests are seldom an issue. Instead, strong wind [4] dries the soil out rapidly, meaning automatic drip watering is essential.

I would like to learn how to play the acoustic guitar. I would start with short online lessons and practise for twenty minutes every evening after dinner. A practical skill I have been keen to pick up for quite a while is video editing. In my studies and teaching work, clear visual explanations make complex topics far easier to grasp, so being able to produce crisp three-minute explainer clips would be genuinely useful. To learn it systematically, I would enrol in a structured beginner course and set aside twenty minutes every evening to edit short clips I film on my phone. The toughest part at the beginning would definitely be the steep learning curve — timeline software always looks intimidating until keyboard shortcuts become second nature. 📄 Band 6.0 vs Band 7.5+ Part 3 Models — open AFTER you try
Band 6.0 Starter

I think online courses can be very effective for independent students. This is because learners can replay difficult lectures at any time. For example, working adults can study late at night after work. However, younger students usually learn better with a teacher in a real classroom.

Band 7.5+ Model · abstract framing + hedging + contrast

To a large extent, online courses can match classroom teaching when the goal is transmitting information, since self-motivated adults can pause complex lectures and review at their own pace. A good illustration is professionals upskilling in data analysis after work hours. That said, education involves far more than absorbing content: hands-on subjects, spontaneous debate, and immediate corrective feedback still work noticeably better face to face, particularly for younger learners who benefit from routine and peer accountability.

5 strategies that help most

1 · Questions first

Read the questions before the passage so you scan with a clear purpose.

2 · Hunt names, dates & terms

3 · Same meaning, different words

Correct answers almost always paraphrase the passage using synonyms.

Passage 3 is usually the most abstract — never let Passage 1 eat 30 minutes!

5 · Check question order

T/F/NG, Y/N/NG, MCQ, Sentence Completion & Short Answers follow text order; Matching Headings/Info do not!

TRUE/FALSE vs YES/NO

Band 6.5 Complete Essay · 258 words · 4 balanced paragraphs

Digital technology has made online study widely available, leading to debate over whether virtual courses teach as effectively as traditional classroom lessons. While online learning works very well for self-motivated adults, I believe face-to-face classes remain more effective overall for most students.

On the one hand, online courses offer clear practical advantages, particularly flexibility. Learners can watch recorded lectures at any hour and pause or replay difficult explanations until they understand them fully. For example, full-time employees or parents with family duties can complete a postgraduate certificate late in the evening without commuting to a campus. Without online options, many of these learners would not be able to continue education at all.

On the other hand, physical classrooms provide human interaction and structure that screens struggle to replace. When a teacher notices puzzled expressions in a room, they can adjust their explanation immediately and encourage shy students to ask questions. Studying alongside classmates also builds daily routine and teamwork skills, whereas isolated online learners frequently lose motivation and drop out before finishing their course.

In conclusion, although online courses are an effective tool for disciplined learners with busy schedules, classroom teaching delivers stronger support, feedback, and engagement for the majority of students.

Band 7.5+ Complete Essay · 279 words · Hedging, Concession & Varied Complex Clauses

Rapid improvements in streaming platforms have prompted many educators to argue that online courses now rival traditional classroom teaching. Although virtual study can be remarkably effective for transmitting factual content to disciplined adults, I would argue that face-to-face classrooms remain superior wherever deeper discussion, practical skill-building, and learner accountability are required.

Admittedly, well-designed online programmes offer flexibility that physical timetables cannot match. Learners can revisit dense lectures at their own pace and fit study around employment or caregiving responsibilities. A hospital nurse preparing for a specialist qualification, for instance, can review recorded seminars between shifts rather than spending hours commuting. Where the objective is mastering clearly structured content independently, virtual delivery appears to perform just as well as lecture halls.

Nevertheless, effective education rarely consists solely of absorbing information. In a physical classroom, skilled teachers read subtle non-verbal cues, clarify misconceptions on the spot, and guide spontaneous debate among peers with competing viewpoints. Hands-on fields — from laboratory sciences to schoolteacher training — likewise depend on immediate feedback that video calls struggle to replicate. Equally important, scheduled in-person attendance creates social accountability, which helps explain why completion rates in purely self-paced online courses tend to lag behind campus programmes.

In conclusion, while online courses provide an invaluable pathway for self-directed learners seeking flexibility, they complement rather than replace the interactive depth of classroom teaching.

🇯🇵 ↔ 🇬🇧 3 crucial differences every Japanese learner should know:
  • Grammar boxes don't match 1-to-1: Japanese and English categories do not line up perfectly, so word-by-word translation causes mistakes.
  • Articles (Kanshi / 冠詞: a, an, the) don't exist in Japanese: that is why forgetting a / an / the is the #1 habit to watch in IELTS Writing, Speaking, and note completion!

Step B · All 5 Core Patterns + 3 Useful Extensions / 5文型＋3つの広げ方

Pattern letters: S = Subject (主語: who/what the sentence is about) · V = Verb (動詞: action or state) · O = Object (目的語: person/thing affected) · C = Complement (補語: describes/identifies S or O) · IO = Indirect Object (間接目的語: person receiving) · DO = Direct Object (直接目的語: thing given/sent).

🛠️ Lesson 4 · Top 5 Japanese→English Traps Examiners Notice Immediately 5 MIN · OPTIONAL
🎯 Instant score boost: Fixing just these 5 high-frequency habits cleans up over half the grammar slips examiners hear and read from Japanese test-takers!
日本の受験生が一番やりがちな5つのミスです。これを知るだけで減点が一気に減ります！

1 · Double verb (be + base verb)

✗ I am play soccer.
✓ I play soccer.

Use be before a noun/adjective (I am happy), not before a regular base action verb.

2 · “I am agree” trap (Dōshi vs Keiyōshi)

✗ I am agree with this view.
✓ I agree with this view.

agree, happen, belong, depend are Verbs, not adjectives — never put am / is / are in front!

3 · Naked singular noun (missing Kanshi)

✗ Student needs notebook.
✓ A student needs a notebook. (or Students need notebooks.)

A countable singular noun never stands naked — add a / an / the / my or make it plural (-s)!

4 · Missing subject It / They (Daimeishi)

✗ Because is convenient.
✓ Because it is convenient.

Japanese omits それ, but every English clause must have a Subject (S + V) — even after because!

5 · Particle (を・について) trap

✗ discuss about a problem / listen music
✓ discuss a problem / listen to music

discuss takes an Object directly (S + V + O); listen needs the preposition to!

Bonus · Lone “Because…” fragment

✗ I like online courses. Because I can save time.
✓ I like online courses because I can save time.

In IELTS Writing, attach a because / although clause to a main clause with no period between them!
